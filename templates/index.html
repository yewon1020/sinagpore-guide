About Singapore

  Singapore is an island and city-state located in Southeast Asia, south of the Malay Peninsula. It is a multicultural society where English, Chinese, Malay, and Tamil are the official languages and diverse races coexist, including Chinese, Malay, and Indian. It is a tropical climate with high temperatures and humidity, and is famous for its strict rule of law, well-developed infrastructure, and clean environment.


Also, Singapore has many famous places.


Warnings

There are some warnings before you go to Singapore.


No chewing gum in and out: Only nicotine gum (for non-smoking supplements) and dental gum (for medical purposes) are allowed in Singapore as an exception. In addition, if you spit out your gum or take out your trash in public, you will be fined 300 SGD (about 270,000 won) per catch, and repeated violations can double the fine

Restrictive regulation on waste dumping: General waste dumping alone can result in a fine of 300 SGD (about 320,000 won), a fine of up to 2,000 SGD (about 2.16 million won) for repeated detection, and a correction labor order. Be careful not to throw away even a small piece of tissue!

No Food On Public Transport: Food intake is completely prohibited on MRTs (subways) and buses. All food intake, including water, is prohibited, and any caught may result in a fine of 500 SGD (about W540,000).

Using Your Right Hand: In Malay or Indian cultures, it is polite to eat or exchange things with your right hand. This is because the left hand is considered to be the hand used in the bathroom and is considered unclean.

Take Off Your Shoes: You must take off your shoes at the entrance when visiting temples, mosques, and some homes. Especially when visiting Hindu temples or mosques.

Don't Copy Singlish: Singaporean English 'singlish' has unique terminating endings such as "laah," "lor," and "meh." Be careful because even if they are used by locals, tourists may feel mocked if they copy them.
